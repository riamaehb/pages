MODULE 1

Understanding and Organizing Government Data

Basic concepts for working with records and reports.

What is the reporting task?

Consider an office preparing an employment-assistance report. Registration information is in an Excel workbook, placements are recorded in a Word table, and pending or withdrawn cases are in a CSV file. Before reporting totals, the office needs to understand what each file contains and how the records relate.

Basic concepts

TermMeaning in this exercise
RecordOne row representing an applicant registration in the source table.
FieldOne column describing an attribute, such as Applicant_ID or Barangay.
IdentifierA value used to identify or match records, such as APP-0013 in Applicant_ID.
Data typeThe kind of value stored in a field, such as text, number, or date.
Data qualityWhether records are complete, consistent, valid, and sufficiently accurate for the intended report.
Level of detailWhat one row represents. Here, the intended final dataset has one row per applicant.

Example: A narrative and a table

The practice files describe 100 unique applicant registrations, including 82 placed, 11 pending, and 7 withdrawn. These counts can be presented in a short report or in a structured table:

IndicatorValue
Registered100
Placed82
Pending11
Withdrawn7

The totals are derived from the same applicant records used in the later exercises. They describe the practice dataset, not official government statistics.

Questions for discussion

  1. If an applicant appears twice, does that mean two different people registered?
  2. If one file uses “Concep. Grande” and another uses “Concepcion Grande,” what happens when we count applicants by barangay?
  3. What identifier would let us match a registration to its placement record?

Activity

Choose a recurring report from your office. Write down: (1) what question the report answers, (2) which files supply the information, (3) what one row in each file represents, and (4) which field could identify or match records. You do not need to share actual personal data.

Before continuing: You should be able to distinguish a record, a field, an identifier, and a data type. Module 2 introduces the actual practice files.

MODULE 3

Identifying Data Quality Issues

Inspect the source records and decide which issues need correction or review.

What to look for

Before changing a dataset, check whether records are complete, valid, consistent, and unique. An unusual value may be a data-entry error, but it may also be correct. Keep the original records while investigating.

Types of issues

Different problems require different responses. Formatting differences can often be standardized. An abbreviation should be checked against an approved reference. Duplicate identifiers and missing or unexpected values require review before a record is changed or excluded.

IssueExampleResponse
FormattingExtra spacesStandardize in a working column
Inconsistent representationConcep. GrandeCompare with the reference list
Duplicate identifierAPP-0013 appears twiceCompare both records and document a decision
Missing or invalid valueBlank required fieldInvestigate before using the record

MODULE 4

Cleaning and Validating Data

Apply cleaning formulas, review duplicates, and create a validated registration table.

This module has four operations: standardize values, identify records requiring review, record decisions, and create a validated dataset. Follow the steps in order.

Step 1. Make a working copy

  1. In Google Sheets, right-click the Registrations_Raw worksheet tab.
  2. Choose Duplicate.
  3. Rename the new worksheet Registrations_Auto.
  4. Do not edit the original values in columns A–H.
  5. Enter these headings in row 1 of the new sheet: I1 Barangay_Trimmed, J1 Barangay_Clean, K1 Sector_Clean, L1 ID_Count, M1 Duplicate_Review, N1 Barangay_Check, O1 Record_Check, P1 Review_Decision.

Enter the seven formulas below in row 2, one at a time. Leave column P empty until the duplicate-review step.

C. Review and resolve issues

Step 4. Record the duplicate decision

  1. Compare the two APP-0013 records in rows 14 and 102. In this exercise they have identical values.
  2. In Registrations_Auto!P14, enter Retain.
  3. In Registrations_Auto!P102, enter Exclude.
  4. Leave column P blank for the other 99 records.
  5. In Cleaning_Log!E2, replace Pending review with Retain row 14; exclude row 102. In F2, record that both source rows were compared and found identical, and include the reviewer’s name. Keep the source records unchanged.

These are decisions for the supplied practice file. In an actual office dataset, duplicates should be reviewed before deciding which record to keep.

If something does not match

  • Wrong counts: Check that the formulas were filled through row 102 and that P14 is Retain and P102 is Exclude.
  • #REF! in a new worksheet: Make sure the formula has empty cells into which it can expand.
  • ARRAY_LITERAL error: Copy the complete review formula, including the three-value fallback inside IFNA.
  • Formula refers to a missing sheet: Verify the worksheet names Registrations_Auto and Barangay_Reference.
  • Unexpected barangay results: Confirm the spelling of Concepcion Grande and the reference list in Barangay_Reference!A2:A28.

1. Define the indicator

The practice dataset contains 100 registered applicants, of whom 82 are placed, 11 are pending, and 7 are withdrawn. The placement rate is the number placed divided by the number registered: 82 ÷ 100 = 82%. This is the proportion of registered applicants classified as placed in the practice dataset. It is not the city employment rate or a count of jobs generated.

4. Interpret counts and rates

Consider two hypothetical barangays: one has 20 pending cases out of 200 applicants (10%), and another has 10 pending cases out of 40 applicants (25%). The first has more pending cases, but the second has a higher pending proportion. When comparing offices or areas, state both the numerator and denominator, and specify the reporting period.

Question: If you want to identify barangays with the largest number of pending applicants, which fields would you use? In this dataset, Barangay identifies the area, Status identifies pending cases, and Applicant_ID identifies the records to count. Interpreting the chart

The chart shows 82 placed, 11 pending, and 7 withdrawn applicants out of 100 registered applicants in the practice dataset. The largest group is placed, but the chart does not explain why applicants were placed or withdrawn.

Check your interpretation: The placement proportion is 82 ÷ 100 = 82%. This is the proportion of registered applicants recorded as placed in this dataset, not an employment rate for Naga City residents or a measure of jobs created.
